Why I Study Cancer ?

Cancer is often defined by how aggressively it grows. I am more interested in how stubbornly it survives. As a cancer biology graduate student, I study myeloid malignancies and the rare cells that endure treatment, adapt, and seed relapse. Their resilience raises the question that drives my work: can the mechanisms that protect malignant cells also become the key to destroying them? My goal is to uncover these hidden vulnerabilities and help translate them into more precise, durable therapies for patients with blood cancers.

What i'm Interested In?

  • Therapy Resistance

    Drug tolerant persister cell biology, phenotypic plasticity under therapeutic pressure, evolutionary dynamics of acquired resistance, combination/ multi-pathway targeting strategies to overcome resistance

  • Hematological malignancies

    Leukemia stem cell biology, clonal hematopoiesis and leukemic transformation, Bone-marrow niche remodeling, cell-based therapies

  • Cancer Metabolism

    Mitochondrial metabolism and targeted therapies, Metabolic plasticity and nutrient stress, Metabolism-Immunotherapy combinations

  • Tumor Microenvironment

    Tumor-associated macrophage depletion or repolarization, Hypoxia modulation, CXCR4 Axis Inhibition and Niche Disengagement Strategies, Bone marrow microenvironment
